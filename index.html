Phishing

Fraud messages that impersonate trusted sources to steal logins or money.

How to spot it

  • Urgent pressure (“act now or lose access”).
  • Look‑alike sender or domain (tiny typos, extra characters).
  • Links that don’t match the display text.
  • Unexpected attachments (ZIP, EXE, macro docs).

What to do

  • Don’t click links; type the real URL or use a bookmark.
  • Verify through a contact method you find yourself.
  • Report phishing to your email provider and IT/security team.

Malware

Malicious software that can lock, spy, or damage your files and devices.

Common types

  • Ransomware: locks files and demands payment.
  • Spyware/Keyloggers: steals passwords and data.
  • Trojans: disguised as legitimate apps.

Prevention tips

  • Install apps from trusted stores only.
  • Keep OS, browser, and security tools updated.
  • Be cautious with macros and unknown installers.
  • Back up important files regularly.

Social Engineering

Tricks that target human trust: impostors, fake support, urgent favors, and more.

Red flags

  • Urgent requests for help, secrecy, or gift cards.
  • Requests for one‑time codes or passwords (legitimate orgs don’t ask).
  • Pressure to break procedure or skip verification.

How to respond

  • Pause and verify identity using a known contact method.
  • Follow official procedures; involve your IT/security team.
  • Document details if you need to report the attempt.

Public Wi‑Fi Risks

Untrusted networks can expose your traffic to eavesdropping or tampering.

Safer habits

  • Avoid sensitive logins (email, banking) on open networks.
  • Prefer mobile data or use a trusted VPN.
  • Turn off auto‑connect and file sharing in public places.

Signs of trouble

  • Captive portal asks for your email password (not normal).
  • Certificate warnings or mismatched domains.
  • Frequent disconnects or strange redirects.
